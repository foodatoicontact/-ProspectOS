# ProspectOS — acquisition playbook

Write in French, with Kevin's voice (founder, direct, a bit of self-mockery, never corporate). Every claim must be true
and checkable — the product's whole promise is "every fact has a proof". A marketing text that invents a number, a
client or a result breaks the brand. When a fact is unknown, leave a visible `[À CONFIRMER]`, never make one up.

## Positioning (confirm with Kevin before reusing the details)

- **What:** ProspectOS finds the companies worth contacting **now**, each with its public source and the reason to
  contact it (signal: recruiting, recent creation, dated site, public contracts won…). Every proposal is checked by a
  human before it counts.
- **For whom:** freelancers, web/marketing agencies, consultants and B2B SMEs who prospect themselves — first local
  (Occitanie: Toulouse, Gers), then France.
- **Against:** bought contact lists, generic "500 leads" files, LinkedIn automation that spams.
- **Proof points available:** official public data (Sirene register, public procurement DECP, company websites),
  human validation, "we deliver 1 verified prospect rather than 5 approximate ones" (real case, oct. 2026).
- **Offer on the site (checked in the code, 6 Nov. 2026):** free 7-day trial, no credit card — 20 prospect searches,
  50 prospect analyses, 5 AI offer analyses for the whole trial; nothing is billed at the end. Sign-up:
  `https://prospectos.fr/#inscription`; a public demo runs without an account. Re-check `src/domain/pricing.ts` and
  `src/domain/seo-content.ts` before quoting. The October cold-email offer (5 free prospects, 50 for 99 € HT) is not
  on the site: do not quote it unless Kevin confirms it is still valid.
- Kevin also runs **Foodatoi** (ordering platform for restaurants). Never mix both in one piece of content unless the
  bridge is explicit; a reader must always know which product is talked about.

## Kevin's voice — the rule for every post (set on 6 Nov. 2026)

Kevin rejected a post as « du vu et revu » (generic SaaS LinkedIn: hook + 🔹 bullets + « 👉 » CTA stack + "Testez 7
jours"). Every post now follows this voice; when in doubt, read the line aloud: would Kevin say it to a freelancer
over a coffee?
- **Tutoiement**, short sentences, one idea per line. Spoken French, not brochure French (« des boîtes », « un site
  d'un autre siècle »). No corporate words: « solution », « optimiser », « booster », « levier », « game changer ».
- **Self-mockery first, selling second.** Admit the cliché or the weakness before making the point (« Je déteste les
  posts "essai gratuit". Alors voilà le mien. »). Never brag.
- **Talk about the problem the reader lives** (who do I call Monday, and what do I say), then what the product does in
  plain words, with its proof — never feature lists in marketing words.
- **Honesty as the hook:** say what the product does NOT do, or when it finds nothing. It is the brand.
- **No emoji bullets, no « 👉 » stack, no hashtag wall.** Arrows (→) or plain lines; at most one emoji in the post.
- **One or two calls to action, in his words** (« Écris DÉMO », « Lien en commentaire »), never three in a row.
- **The link goes in the first comment**, never in the post.
- Still true and checkable: no invented anecdote, figure or client. A story Kevin has not told yet = `[À CONFIRMER]`.

## Lessons from real data (LinkedIn, oct. 2026)

The WhatsApp-scam story post: 28 000 impressions, 94 % outside the network, 10 comments, **0 saves, 1 follower**,
40 % Paris, restaurants absent from the top sectors, published at 4:19 am.
- Reach ≠ target. Judge a post by **comments from the target, saves, followers and DMs**, not impressions.
- The first line must speak to the target ("Agences web : …", "Freelances : …"), not to everybody.
- Give a reason to **save**: 3 concrete steps, a checklist, a number, a mini-method.
- End with **one precise question** the target can answer.
- Publish 7:30–9:00 or around 12:00; answer every comment in the first hour; never edit in the first hour.
- A sceptic in comments boosts reach: answer once with a proof and humour, never with anger, never with a bet.
- Founder stories work when the lesson lands on the product's promise (trust is proven, not claimed).

## Formats

**LinkedIn post (default):** in Kevin's voice (section above). Hook line for the target → short real story or honest
admission (3–5 lines) → the lesson → what ProspectOS does, in plain words, with its proof → one or two CTAs → one
question. 120–200 words. At most one emoji, no emoji bullets.

**Short video (Reels / TikTok / Shorts / LinkedIn video), 30–45 s, face camera:**
1. 0–3 s hook said AND written on screen ("J'ai refusé de livrer 5 prospects à un client.").
2. 3–25 s the story or the demo, one idea per shot, subtitles burned in (most watch muted).
3. 25–40 s the lesson / the 3 tips.
4. Last 5 s: one call to action ("Commentez PROSPECT pour recevoir 5 prospects gratuits").
Deliver: script with timings, on-screen text per shot, b-roll ideas (screen recording of ProspectOS showing a source
and its excerpt), caption + 3–5 hashtags. Never show private data on screen (contacts, phone numbers, real prospects'
emails): use the demo mode or blur.

**Carousel (LinkedIn PDF):** 6–8 slides, one idea per slide, slide 1 = the promise, last slide = the call to action.

**Cold email / DM:** personal first line from a **verified public fact** about the prospect (with its source), one
question, one offer, an easy "non" exit, an unsubscribe line. Never guess an email address; only public professional
contacts; B2B only (GDPR / CNIL). 80–120 words.

**Lead magnet:** "5 prospects vérifiés gratuits dans votre zone" (sector + zone), delivered with sources — it is the
product itself, the best demo.

## Weekly rhythm (suggested)

- 3 LinkedIn posts: 1 founder story, 1 practical method (savable), 1 proof/case (anonymised, with consent).
- 1 short video repurposing the best post of the week.
- 20 personalised cold messages built from ProspectOS's own proofs.
- Friday: read the stats (target comments, saves, followers, DMs, replies) and adjust the next week.

## Never

- Fake testimonials, invented numbers, "X clients" not true, screenshots of real people without consent.
- Scraping social networks or search engines to find leads; buying contact lists.
- Naming or shaming a commenter; arguing in public.
